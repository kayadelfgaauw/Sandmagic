How It Works

Drawn on the Earth. Seen from the Sky.

Step 01

The Canvas

Selecting the stage—a ripe wheat field at dusk, an open field for human animation, or a wide beach at low tide.

Step 02

Ropes & Geometry

Translating a scale blueprint into hundreds of meters on the ground using wooden pegs and measuring ropes.

Step 03

Grain, Motion & Sand

Flattening grain with stomping boards, choreographing 150+ people frame by frame, or raking wet sand by hand.

Step 04

From the Sky

Only from the air does the full artwork reveal itself—captured by drone or aircraft in photographs and stop-motion film.

Step 05

Given Back

Nothing permanent remains: the farmer harvests the grain, the crowd disperses, and the rising tide smooths the shore.

The Creators

Remko & René

“Whether we are flattening grain at night, moving step by step with a hundred people, or raking sand before the tide comes in—it’s never about keeping what you make. It’s about creating something extraordinary together.”

Two-time Guinness World Record holders (Project Atlas in 2009 and Act As One in 2021). For nearly thirty years, co-creators Remko Delfgaauw and René Bosma have turned wheat fields, open landscapes, and coastlines into monumental art.
