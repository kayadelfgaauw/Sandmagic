Bayer Ordered Dither Engine: Technical Dossier

A technical breakdown of the mathematical calibration, Rec. 709 luma quantization, coordinate warp dynamics, and batch rendering pipeline powering the Sandmagic living web experience.

01 Subject Contrast & The Invert Flag

The most prevalent pitfall in digital halftoning and ordered dither is failing to identify the subject's inherent contrast relative to its ground. In the case of Remko Delfgaauw's sand drawings on the Dutch coast, the subject consists of dark raked sand lines etched into light reflective beach sand.

Without inversion, high luma regions (the empty beach sand) generate maximum dot density, causing the background to flood with dots while leaving the artwork empty. Setting invert = true maps high luma to zero dot density, ensuring the quantized ink particles concentrate exclusively on the raked grooves and human figures.

Luma Inversion Logic: if (invert) L_norm = 1.0 - L_norm;

02 Frame Extraction & Boundary Cleaning

The original footage (gemini_generated_video_4885ad27.mp4) contains an AI-generated parchment border along its exterior margins. Extracting the frames without cropping results in an artificial border dithering into a rigid rectangle in the center of the viewport.

We apply an 11 FPS extraction rate and an 8% geometric margin crop:

ffmpeg -i gemini_generated_video_4885ad27.mp4 \ -vf "fps=11,crop=iw*0.84:ih*0.84:iw*0.08:ih*0.08,scale=1400:-2" \ frames/%03d.png

Noise Floor Suppression: Beach sand naturally carries compression grain and subtle footprints. We apply a normalized floor clamp (bgFloor = 0.02). Any pixel with tone below this threshold is snapped cleanly to zero, completely eliminating codec noise haze.

03 Rec. 709 Luma & Pinned Auto-Leveling

Luminance is calculated using standard HDTV ITU-R Recommendation BT.709-6 spectral weighting coefficients:

Rec. 709 Luma Formula: Luma = 0.2126 × R + 0.7152 × G + 0.0722 × B

The Pinned Auto-Levels Principle: Dynamic per-frame contrast expansion causes the dot density to visibly flicker or pulse as video frames alternate. To eliminate this pulsation, the engine computes the 2nd percentile (\(P_2\)) and 98th percentile (\(P_{98}\)) from the luma histogram on sequence initialization and pins those reference levels across all subsequent frames.

04 Recursive 8×8 Bayer Ordered Quantization

Unlike Floyd–Steinberg error diffusion which produces organic, wandering stipple clusters, ordered dither relies on spatial threshold matrices that yield a crisp, architectural grid.

The 8×8 Bayer matrix is synthesized recursively from a fundamental \(2 \times 2\) seed:

M_2 = [[0, 2], [3, 1]] M_{2n} = [ [4 * M_n, 4 * M_n + 2 * U_n], [4 * M_n + 3 * U_n, 4 * M_n + 1 * U_n] ]

Threshold values are normalized by \(1 / 64\) and offset by \(+0.5\). Quantization maps the adjusted tone into \(N=4\) discrete levels, scaling circle radii to preserve visual airiness.

05 High-Performance Path Bucketing

Rendering thousands of individual circles per frame with unique ctx.fillStyle assignments causes massive JavaScript bridge overhead and drops framerates below 20 FPS.

Our engine groups cells into discrete level and color buckets. In Duotone mode, all cells sharing tone level \(k\) are batched into a single beginPath() and drawn with a single fill() call. This reduces draw calls from 10,000+ to under 15 calls per frame, effortlessly sustaining 60+ FPS.

06 Sampling Coordinate Warping

Instead of displacing drawn pixels after rendering, pointer interaction displaces the sampling coordinates when reading from the downscaled offscreen buffer. Because this occurs during array lookup, the interaction adds zero extra draw passes.

We employ a Rotational Twist across a 60% viewport radius with cubic smoothstep falloff (\(s^2(3 - 2s)\)), creating a field effect that responds organically to the radial geometry of the mandala without artifacting.
